## *Which open source project would be useful in your major, and why?*

Zotero is an open source project that helps users collect, organize, cite, and even share their research sources. It allows users to save bibliographic information directly from websites, organize sources into collections, and automatically generate citations and bibliographies in different referencing styles. Zotero was initially created at the  Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media at George Mason University, and currently developed by the Corporation for Digital Scholarship (Zotero, n.d.-a). 

Zotero is a great reference management software, it is able to automatically collect source information when searching for articles on AU Library, JSTOR, etc. Sorting your references into subcategories for each course, project, or assignment, is really helpful for ease of navigation and overview. It is also really easy to export and import a library of sources between users or devices. While Aarhus University grants students license to a similar reference management software, EndNote, by using Zotero you are able to keep your library of sources used throughout university even after graduating.  

Zotero is licenced under the AGPLv3 license. This license allows user to use, copy, modify, and distribute the software freely. However, it requires modified versions available over a network to make their source code available to its users as well (GNU Operating System, 2007). The Zotero project remains very active, with its initial release in 2006 and currently experiencing commits on GitHub daily and issues continuing to be resolved. The most recent commit being yesterday, on 11th November, as of writing. It has a total of 90 contributors on the main Zotero project page. Initially, Zotero was funded by Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, the United States Institute of Museum and Library Services, and the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation (Zotero, n.d.-a). Currently, Zotero is instead funded by individual and institutional storage subscriptions (Zotero, n.d.-b). 

Personally, I recommend Zotero to anyone looking to do a paper or research in general, and something I will rely on for my own thesis. It is a very intuitive and efficient reference management system that continues to be improved upon. While I could leave the program, and use a different similar one, I would not want to.
